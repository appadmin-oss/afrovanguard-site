## Preamble

**We, the people of Afrovanguard,**

conscious of our African identity, our common humanity, our cultural inheritance, and our responsibility to generations yet unborn;

recognising that the greatness of a people is measured not merely by the abundance of their resources, the magnificence of their institutions, or the achievements of a privileged few, but by the character, competence, dignity, and collective advancement of their people;

acknowledging that corruption, ignorance, poverty, cultural dislocation, technological dependency, irresponsible leadership, and the abandonment of communal responsibility have weakened the foundations of our societies;

affirming that no community can sustainably advance beyond the values it cultivates, the knowledge it preserves, the leadership it produces, and the institutions it builds;

determined to raise an incorruptible generation of African leaders who shall preserve what is noble in our heritage, reform what no longer serves humanity, and introduce to the world the excellence, ingenuity, and possibilities of African civilisation;

resolved that leadership shall arise through service, authority shall be restrained by accountability, knowledge shall be transmitted through mentorship, and institutional power shall never become the inheritance of a person, family, class, or privileged circle;

committed to building enduring institutions through education, technology, research, culture, enterprise, creative expression, professional development, and community participation; and

recognising that every child deserves opportunity, every talent deserves cultivation, every community deserves responsible leadership, and every generation bears the obligation to improve the inheritance it received;

**do hereby ordain and establish this Constitution for Afrovanguard** as the supreme internal instrument governing its membership, leadership, institutions, projects, resources, conduct, accountability, and succession, subject always to the laws of the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

## Part One — Foundations

### Article I — The Institution

#### Section 1. Name

(1) The name of the institution is **Ambassadors for Community, Tech and Cultural Advancements**, referred to in this Constitution as **"Afrovanguard"**.

(2) Afrovanguard was founded as Okun Alimosho on 9 November 2017.

#### Section 2. Legal status and character

(1) Afrovanguard is an independent, non-profit, non-partisan, community-centred institution dedicated to the moral, intellectual, cultural, technological, economic, and social advancement of African communities.

(2) Afrovanguard shall maintain its registered legal identity and may operate programmes, centres, chapters, academies, and other approved establishments in Nigeria and internationally, subject to applicable law.

#### Section 3. Motto

(1) The motto of Afrovanguard is **"The Force for Good."**

(2) The motto represents the obligation of every Member to use knowledge, influence, resources, ability, and opportunity for the advancement of humanity.

#### Section 4. Vision

To raise an incorruptible generation of African leaders and establish sustainable institutions through which African communities may achieve cultural dignity, technological independence, economic prosperity, responsible governance, and holistic human development.

#### Section 5. Mission

Afrovanguard shall pursue its vision by:

- (a) preserving, promoting, and protecting the values of African communities;
- (b) raising children and young people who demonstrate character, competence, responsibility, and leadership;
- (c) advancing African talent, research, technology, innovation, and enterprise;
- (d) strengthening parenting, family formation, and communal responsibility;
- (e) preserving languages, cultural knowledge, indigenous institutions, and responsible relationships with land and nature;
- (f) developing ethical professionals, entrepreneurs, educators, researchers, artists, and community leaders;
- (g) establishing accountable, self-sustaining community institutions; and
- (h) mobilising partnerships and resources for measurable community advancement.

#### Section 6. Institutional independence

(1) Afrovanguard is not owned by its founder, Trustees, Directors, donors, Patrons, employees, volunteers, or Members.

(2) Its property and resources shall be held and administered exclusively for its lawful non-profit purposes.

(3) No office, rank, title, financial contribution, or family relationship confers any proprietary right over Afrovanguard.

#### Section 7. Supremacy of this Constitution

(1) This Constitution is supreme over every internal regulation, operational policy, appointment instrument, administrative directive, and personal instruction issued within Afrovanguard.

(2) No individual has authority to suspend or disregard this Constitution.

(3) Where this Constitution conflicts with applicable law or with Afrovanguard's legally effective registered governing instrument, the law and the registered instrument prevail until lawfully amended.

### Article II — Interpretation and Definitions

#### Section 1. Definitions

In this Constitution, unless the context requires otherwise:

- **"Academy"** means Afrovanguard Academy established under Article XII.
- **"Approved Trainer"** means a person certified by the Academy to conduct formation under the Rank and Formation Schedule.
- **"Board"** means the Board of Directors established under Article IX.
- **"Bylaws"** means the subsidiary instruments made under Article XXII.
- **"CACENTRE"** means a community centre established under Article XII, Section 3.
- **"Chief Officer"** means any officer established under Article X, Sections 4 to 13.
- **"Council"** means the Governing Council established under Article VII.
- **"Directorate"** means one of the seven Community Advancement Directorates established under Article XI.
- **"Evaluated Academy Session"** means a teaching or facilitation session delivered in the Academy and assessed under Academy rules.
- **"Founder"** means the person or persons who founded Afrovanguard as Okun Alimosho.
- **"General Assembly"** means the assembly of voting Members established under Article VI.
- **"Member"** means a person admitted to any membership class under Article IV.
- **"President"** means the President and Chief Servant under Article X.
- **"Project Director"** means the head of a Directorate, the Academy, or another approved project under Article XIII.
- **"Rank"** means a level of the O–G formation system under Article V.
- **"Registered Constitution"** means the constitution of Afrovanguard as filed with the Corporate Affairs Commission.
- **"Trustees"** means the registered Trustees of Afrovanguard under Article VIII.
- **"Two-Trainer Formation"** means supervised formation completed under at least two independent Approved Trainers.
- **"Verified Active Membership"** means continuous, documented participation in an approved Afrovanguard project, verified under the Membership Bylaws.

#### Section 2. Rules of interpretation

(1) "Shall" and "must" are mandatory. "May" is permissive.

(2) Headings and the table of contents are for convenience only and do not affect interpretation.

(3) Words in the singular include the plural and vice versa. Every reference to a person applies equally to persons of any gender.

(4) A reference to a law includes that law as amended or re-enacted.

(5) Where this Constitution requires a matter to be settled by Bylaws or a Schedule, the matter has no operative effect beyond what this Constitution states until that Bylaw or Schedule is lawfully approved.

### Article III — The Seven Foundational Values

#### Section 1. Declaration of values

Afrovanguard is founded on seven inseparable values: **Individuation, Faith, Diligence, Accountability, Responsibility, Cultural Appreciation, and Communal Spirit.**

#### Section 2. Individuation

(1) Every person shall be encouraged to discover, develop, and responsibly express their unique abilities, identity, intelligence, and purpose.

(2) No Member shall be required to surrender independent thought, conscience, or creativity as a condition of loyalty.

#### Section 3. Faith

(1) Faith represents principled conviction, hope, trustworthiness, and commitment to purpose.

(2) Afrovanguard respects freedom of religion, belief, and conscience, and shall not impose religious conformity as a condition of ordinary membership or of access to community programmes.

#### Section 4. Diligence

(1) Every Member shall cultivate excellence through learning, disciplined effort, preparation, and consistent execution.

(2) Positions of leadership shall not be awarded merely for popularity, seniority, or proximity to influential persons.

#### Section 5. Accountability

(1) Every officeholder shall answer for the authority exercised, the resources entrusted, the decisions made, and the outcomes produced.

(2) No officeholder is above investigation, evaluation, or lawful removal.

#### Section 6. Responsibility

Every Member shall recognise the relationship between personal choices, communal welfare, institutional development, and future generations.

#### Section 7. Cultural Appreciation

Afrovanguard shall preserve and advance African languages, traditions, knowledge systems, histories, institutions, arts, and indigenous wisdom, while rejecting practices that violate human dignity or applicable law.

#### Section 8. Communal Spirit

(1) The advancement of the individual shall be pursued alongside the advancement of the community.

(2) Service, cooperation, mentorship, mutual respect, and shared responsibility are essential expressions of membership.

## Part Two — Membership and Formation

### Article IV — Membership

#### Section 1. Classes of membership

(1) Afrovanguard recognises the following classes of membership:

| Class | Status |
|---|---|
| Platform Member | Registered supporter and introductory participant |
| Regular Project Member | Active participant in an approved project |
| Premium Project Member | Active project member with additional approved benefits or commitments |
| Official Volunteer | Verified volunteer with assigned responsibilities |
| Executive Volunteer | Volunteer with formal operational leadership duties |
| Full Member | Member satisfying the full membership qualifications set in the Membership Bylaws |
| Life Member | Member granted continuing membership under the Membership Bylaws |

(2) The qualifications, admission procedure, and voting eligibility of each class shall be set in the Membership Bylaws.

(3) Honorary Patrons and external advisers do not acquire ordinary voting rights solely by accepting an honorary appointment.

#### Section 2. Rights of Members

Subject to this Constitution and the Bylaws, every Member has the right to:

- (a) participate in eligible programmes;
- (b) receive clear information about their membership obligations;
- (c) apply for available training and leadership opportunities;
- (d) submit proposals, complaints, and petitions;
- (e) receive fair treatment without unlawful discrimination;
- (f) access appropriate institutional reports and disclosures;
- (g) vote in elections where their membership class confers voting eligibility; and
- (h) due process in disciplinary proceedings.

#### Section 3. Obligations of Members

Every Member shall:

- (a) uphold the seven foundational values;
- (b) observe Afrovanguard's safeguarding and ethical standards;
- (c) fulfil accepted service and financial obligations;
- (d) keep their membership information accurate;
- (e) respect institutional property and confidential information;
- (f) refrain from corruption, exploitation, harassment, and abuse of office; and
- (g) contribute to communal advancement according to their capacity and commitments.

#### Section 4. Membership before governance

(1) No person shall become a voting Director or a registered Trustee without first completing at least twelve consecutive months of Verified Active Membership in an approved Afrovanguard project.

(2) No donation, celebrity endorsement, political office, traditional title, professional qualification, or personal relationship may substitute for this requirement.

#### Section 5. Institutional formation for governing offices

(1) In addition to Section 4, every candidate for the Board or for registered Trusteeship shall:

- (a) complete Two-Trainer Formation;
- (b) teach or facilitate at least two Evaluated Academy Sessions;
- (c) pass an assessment of institutional values, governance, ethics, and responsibilities;
- (d) demonstrate a satisfactory record of service and accountability; and
- (e) complete conflict-of-interest and eligibility declarations.

(2) These requirements are mandatory for every new appointment made after this Constitution is lawfully adopted. Existing appointments are governed by Article XXV, Section 2.

### Article V — The O–G Formation System

#### Section 1. Establishment

(1) Afrovanguard shall maintain a progressive formation and leadership system of eight Ranks, O to G.

(2) The system measures institutional maturity, not financial wealth or social status.

| Rank | Designation | Principal qualification |
|---|---|---|
| O | Observer | Orientation and introduction |
| A | Associate | Demonstrated participation |
| B | Builder | Sustained practical service |
| C | Coordinator | Supervised leadership |
| D | Director-Level Formation | Proven institutional leadership |
| E | Executive | Advanced organisational stewardship |
| F | Fellow | Exceptional mentorship and institutional contribution |
| G | Guardian | Distinguished long-term custodianship |

(3) The competencies, training requirements, service thresholds, and promotion procedures for each Rank shall be set in the Rank and Formation Schedule, administered by the Academy and maintained by the Chief People and Culture Officer.

#### Section 2. Rank requirements for office

(1) A voting member of the Governing Council shall have attained at least Rank D.

(2) A Project Director shall ordinarily have attained at least Rank C, with Rank D preferred.

(3) Members of Ranks E, F, and G are eligible for higher custodial responsibilities, subject to election, competence, and ethical standing.

#### Section 3. No automatic promotion

(1) No Rank is acquired solely through the passage of time, donation, employment, family relationship, or appointment to an external office.

(2) Every promotion requires documented evidence and independent assessment.

#### Section 4. Appeals

A Member denied promotion may request a review by an independent Formation and Standards Committee.

## Part Three — Governance

### Article VI — The General Assembly

#### Section 1. Establishment

(1) There shall be a General Assembly of Afrovanguard comprising the Members qualified to participate under the Membership Bylaws.

(2) The General Assembly is the principal constitutional membership forum of Afrovanguard and exercises the powers conferred on it by this Constitution and applicable law.

#### Section 2. Powers

The General Assembly shall:

- (a) consider and approve constitutional amendments;
- (b) elect or ratify eligible governing officers where prescribed;
- (c) receive the annual institutional and financial reports;
- (d) consider major questions of institutional direction;
- (e) approve fundamental changes to Afrovanguard's structure where required;
- (f) exercise its lawful powers concerning the appointment and removal of registered Trustees; and
- (g) hold governing bodies accountable through established procedures.

#### Section 3. Meetings

(1) The General Assembly shall meet at least once in every calendar year.

(2) An extraordinary meeting may be convened on a resolution of the competent governing body or on a qualifying petition of voting Members, as set in the Bylaws.

#### Section 4. Voting

(1) Each eligible voting Member has one vote.

(2) Voting eligibility is not increased by donations, membership dues, professional status, or seniority.

(3) Notice, quorum, proxy voting, electronic voting, and election procedures shall be set in the Bylaws, consistent with applicable law.

### Article VII — The Governing Council

#### Section 1. Establishment and role

(1) There shall be a Governing Council charged with safeguarding Afrovanguard's vision, philosophy, values, constitutional identity, long-term strategy, and continuity.

(2) The Council is Afrovanguard's senior strategic and constitutional governance body, within the powers lawfully assigned to it.

(3) The Council shall not assume the legal functions of the registered Trustees or the executive functions of management.

#### Section 2. Composition

(1) The Council shall comprise seven to eleven voting members, elected or appointed through the process set in the Election Bylaws.

(2) Once the formation system has matured sufficiently to make it practicable, at least two-thirds of Council members shall hold Rank E or above.

#### Section 3. Qualifications

Every Council member shall:

- (a) hold at least Rank D;
- (b) have completed at least three years of Verified Active Membership;
- (c) have demonstrated leadership in at least one approved Afrovanguard project;
- (d) have completed Two-Trainer Formation and two Evaluated Academy Sessions;
- (e) have satisfactory records of integrity, service, and accountability;
- (f) have the competence to assess institutional strategy and governance; and
- (g) have no unresolved disqualifying conflict of interest.

#### Section 4. Powers

The Council shall:

- (a) approve institutional strategic plans covering three to ten years;
- (b) safeguard the seven foundational values;
- (c) review the performance of the Board and of executive leadership;
- (d) oversee the leadership formation and succession framework;
- (e) recommend institutional policies and constitutional reforms;
- (f) evaluate proposed major expansions, affiliations, and restructuring;
- (g) commission independent governance reviews; and
- (h) ensure that decision-making does not become concentrated in one individual or family.

#### Section 5. Term of office

(1) Council members serve four-year terms and may be re-elected once consecutively.

(2) Terms shall be staggered to preserve continuity.

#### Section 6. Chairperson

(1) The Council shall elect a Chairperson and a Deputy Chairperson from among its eligible members.

(2) The Chairperson presides over meetings and ensures that Council resolutions are implemented through the proper institutional channels.

(3) The Chairperson shall not independently exercise the collective powers of the Council.

#### Section 7. Removal

(1) A Council member may be removed for serious misconduct, persistent non-performance, incapacity, a disqualifying conflict of interest, or substantial violation of this Constitution.

(2) Removal requires an impartial procedure, an opportunity to respond, and approval by the body designated in the Election Bylaws.

### Article VIII — The Registered Trustees

#### Section 1. Establishment and role

(1) Afrovanguard shall maintain registered Trustees in accordance with the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 and other applicable Nigerian law.

(2) The Trustees safeguard the legal continuity, lawful property interests, and statutory responsibilities of Afrovanguard.

#### Section 2. Eligibility

No new registered Trustee shall be appointed without:

- (a) at least twelve consecutive months of Verified Active Membership;
- (b) completion of Two-Trainer Formation;
- (c) two Evaluated Academy Sessions;
- (d) a satisfactory institutional service record;
- (e) completion of governance and fiduciary training; and
- (f) compliance with all statutory eligibility requirements.

#### Section 3. Composition and tenure

(1) There shall be five to nine registered Trustees, subject to the Registered Constitution and applicable law.

(2) Trustees serve four-year terms, renewable once, unless the Registered Constitution lawfully provides otherwise.

#### Section 4. Fiduciary duties

(1) Trustees shall act in good faith and in the best interests of Afrovanguard.

(2) Trustees shall protect institutional property, maintain compliance with applicable law, prevent improper private benefit, and exercise due care in matters within their authority.

#### Section 5. Prohibition of private ownership

(1) No Trustee may claim personal ownership of Afrovanguard's assets by reason of registration, trusteeship, donation, or length of service.

(2) Trustees shall not distribute institutional assets to themselves, the Founder, relatives, or other private persons, except for lawful, properly authorised payments consistent with non-profit restrictions.

#### Section 6. Honorary Institutional Custodians

(1) Afrovanguard may appoint Honorary Institutional Custodians to provide counsel, influence, mentorship, or support.

(2) Honorary Institutional Custodians are not registered Trustees and hold no statutory trustee powers.

### Article IX — The Board of Directors

#### Section 1. Establishment and role

(1) There shall be a Board of Directors responsible for organisational governance, executive oversight, programme performance, risk management, and the effective implementation of institutional strategy.

(2) The Board is an internal governance organ of an incorporated-trustee institution. It is not the statutory board of a company limited by shares.

#### Section 2. Composition

(1) The Board shall comprise seven to eleven voting Directors.

(2) A majority of Directors shall be non-executive, holding no paid executive management position.

(3) The President may attend and participate in Board meetings under the Bylaws, but shall not chair the Board.

#### Section 3. Eligibility

(1) Every voting Director shall satisfy Article IV, Sections 4 and 5.

(2) Every voting Director shall also demonstrate competence in at least one of: education, technology, finance, law, governance, community development, culture, enterprise, research, media, or institutional management.

#### Section 4. Powers

The Board shall:

- (a) approve annual operating plans and budgets within its delegated authority;
- (b) evaluate the President and senior management;
- (c) monitor programme delivery and financial performance;
- (d) establish risk-management and safeguarding standards;
- (e) approve organisational policies and delegated spending limits;
- (f) review quarterly institutional performance reports;
- (g) establish and supervise Board committees; and
- (h) recommend major strategic decisions to the appropriate constitutional authority.

#### Section 5. Committees

(1) The Board shall establish, as necessary:

- (a) an Audit, Finance and Risk Committee;
- (b) a Governance, Nominations and Succession Committee;
- (c) a Programmes, Impact and Safeguarding Committee;
- (d) a Technology, Research and Data Ethics Committee; and
- (e) a Partnerships and Resource Mobilisation Committee.

(2) Each committee shall have written terms of reference and shall report to the Board.

#### Section 6. Term of office

(1) Voting Directors serve two-year terms, renewable once consecutively.

(2) Each Director's performance shall be reviewed annually.

(3) A term confers no automatic right of reappointment.

#### Section 7. Separation of governance and management

(1) No Director may, without a lawful delegation, independently direct employees, authorise unbudgeted expenditure, or interfere in daily project administration.

(2) Project Directors are executive or programme management officers and are distinct from voting Directors of the Board.

## Part Four — The Executive

### Article X — The President and the Executive

#### Section 1. Executive authority

(1) The executive administration of Afrovanguard is vested in the President and Chief Servant, supported by the Chief Operating Officer and the other Chief Officers established under this Article.

(2) The President is the chief executive leader of Afrovanguard, subject to Board oversight, this Constitution, and the lawful powers of the registered Trustees.

#### Section 2. The President and Chief Servant

(1) The President shall:

- (a) lead the execution of Afrovanguard's mission and approved strategic plans;
- (b) represent Afrovanguard in authorised public and institutional engagements;
- (c) coordinate the executive leadership team;
- (d) present annual strategic plans, budgets, and performance reports;
- (e) supervise the Chief Officers and programme delivery;
- (f) promote partnerships and organisational sustainability;
- (g) protect institutional culture and encourage leadership development; and
- (h) ensure compliance with governing resolutions and approved policies.

(2) The President shall not independently amend this Constitution, appoint registered Trustees, transfer institutional property, override financial controls, or designate a successor.

(3) The President serves a four-year term, renewable once consecutively, and is subject to annual performance review. Transitional arrangements for the incumbent Founder are set in Article XXV, Section 3.

#### Section 3. Common provisions for Chief Officers

(1) Every Chief Officer shall be appointed through a transparent, competency-based process approved by the Board, on the basis of institutional alignment, professional competence, integrity, and the ability to develop successors.

(2) An executive appointment does not confer a voting seat on the Board or registered Trusteeship.

(3) Chief Officers serve under written appointment agreements, ordinarily on two-year performance cycles. Continuation depends on organisational need, performance, lawful employment arrangements, and Board-approved procedures.

(4) **Combination of offices.** Until Afrovanguard can sustainably finance separate appointments, the Board may assign the duties of more than one Chief Officer to one qualified person, provided that:

- (a) the finance, compliance, and safeguarding functions each remain independent of the person who approves or executes the transactions or activities they oversee; and
- (b) every combination is recorded in writing and reviewed at least annually.

#### Section 4. Chief Operating Officer (COO)

(1) The COO coordinates the daily operations of Afrovanguard and is the principal operational deputy to the President.

(2) The COO shall:

- (a) translate approved strategy into annual, quarterly, and monthly execution plans;
- (b) coordinate all Directorates and institutional departments;
- (c) supervise the operational performance of CACENTREs and community programmes;
- (d) establish and enforce administrative procedures;
- (e) monitor project deadlines, budgets, staffing, and delivery;
- (f) supervise project reporting and operational dashboards;
- (g) coordinate procurement and logistics within approved limits;
- (h) ensure cross-project collaboration and prevent duplication;
- (i) maintain operational continuity during the authorised absence of the President; and
- (j) present monthly institutional performance reports.

(3) All Project Directors report operationally to the COO unless the Board approves a different arrangement.

(4) The COO shall not override the independent control functions of the CFO, compliance officers, or internal audit.

#### Section 5. Chief Financial Officer (CFO)

(1) The CFO is responsible for financial planning, reporting, stewardship, and control.

(2) The CFO shall:

- (a) prepare consolidated annual budgets and financial forecasts;
- (b) maintain financial records and management accounts;
- (c) supervise the central finance team and Project Finance Officers;
- (d) reconcile donations, dues, grants, programme revenue, and expenses;
- (e) manage cash-flow forecasts and institutional liquidity;
- (f) establish financial controls and payment-authorisation procedures;
- (g) monitor restricted grants and donor-designated funds;
- (h) coordinate external audits and statutory financial reporting;
- (i) advise on the financial viability of expansion projects; and
- (j) present financial reports to the President and the Board.

(3) The CFO has direct access to the Audit, Finance and Risk Committee on material financial concerns.

(4) No executive may require the CFO to conceal, alter, or misrepresent financial information.

#### Section 6. Chief Technology Officer (CTO)

(1) The CTO is responsible for institutional technology, digital infrastructure, cybersecurity, data systems, and technology strategy.

(2) The CTO shall:

- (a) establish the technology architecture and standards;
- (b) supervise Afrovanguard's digital platforms and infrastructure;
- (c) lead cybersecurity, system resilience, access management, and disaster recovery;
- (d) coordinate approved software engineering and digital transformation projects;
- (e) maintain secure membership, volunteer, finance, learning, and project information systems;
- (f) develop policies on data protection, privacy, and responsible AI;
- (g) support the digital requirements of TECHOME and other Directorates;
- (h) review major technology procurements and external technical partnerships;
- (i) establish digital training and technical support standards; and
- (j) report material technology risks to the appropriate Board committee.

(3) The CTO shall not authorise access to confidential financial, beneficiary, or personal information outside approved controls.

#### Section 7. Chief Programmes and Impact Officer (CPIO)

(1) The CPIO is responsible for the quality, relevance, and measurable outcomes of Afrovanguard's programmes.

(2) The CPIO shall:

- (a) establish impact-measurement standards;
- (b) coordinate monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning;
- (c) review programme effectiveness;
- (d) make evidence-based recommendations for programme improvement;
- (e) maintain institutional impact reports; and
- (f) ensure beneficiary feedback informs programme design.

(3) The CPIO works with the COO but retains professional independence in reporting programme results.

#### Section 8. Chief People and Culture Officer (CPCO)

The CPCO is responsible for people development, volunteer systems, human resources, organisational culture, and leadership formation, and shall:

- (a) maintain staff and volunteer policies;
- (b) coordinate recruitment and induction;
- (c) oversee training and performance management;
- (d) maintain the O–G formation system;
- (e) support leadership succession planning;
- (f) protect fair employment and volunteer practices; and
- (g) administer grievance procedures, subject to independent safeguards.

#### Section 9. Chief Partnerships and Development Officer (CPDO)

The CPDO is responsible for fundraising, institutional partnerships, grants, sponsorships, and resource mobilisation, and shall:

- (a) develop annual resource mobilisation plans;
- (b) maintain donor and partnership pipelines;
- (c) coordinate grant applications and reporting;
- (d) cultivate institutional and international partnerships;
- (e) develop corporate and philanthropic sponsorship arrangements;
- (f) ensure partnership agreements align with Afrovanguard's values;
- (g) monitor donor commitments and stewardship; and
- (h) work with the CFO to prevent misleading fundraising representations.

#### Section 10. Chief Communications and Public Affairs Officer (CCO)

The CCO is responsible for institutional communications, brand governance, public relations, media strategy, and public accountability, and shall:

- (a) protect institutional brand identity;
- (b) approve official communication standards;
- (c) supervise public messaging and media relations;
- (d) coordinate crisis communication;
- (e) maintain publication and editorial policies;
- (f) oversee communications strategy in collaboration with MEDIAPRO; and
- (g) ensure that images and stories involving children or vulnerable beneficiaries are published only under appropriate consent and safeguarding rules.

#### Section 11. Chief Research and Strategy Officer (CRSO)

The CRSO is responsible for institutional research, long-term planning, policy analysis, and strategic intelligence, and shall:

- (a) lead research into community needs;
- (b) support long-term institutional planning;
- (c) establish research quality and ethics standards;
- (d) coordinate policy and development studies;
- (e) maintain institutional knowledge resources; and
- (f) support the evaluation of expansion opportunities.

#### Section 12. General Counsel and Compliance Officer

(1) Afrovanguard shall appoint or retain qualified legal and compliance expertise.

(2) The responsible officer shall:

- (a) advise on legal obligations and governance procedures;
- (b) maintain regulatory compliance registers;
- (c) review material contracts;
- (d) coordinate conflict-of-interest disclosures;
- (e) advise on employment, intellectual property, privacy, and safeguarding obligations; and
- (f) report significant legal risks to the appropriate governance body.

(3) Where Afrovanguard cannot support a full-time General Counsel, it may retain an external legal adviser and designate an internal compliance officer.

#### Section 13. Chief Safeguarding and Welfare Officer

(1) There shall be a designated officer responsible for safeguarding children, vulnerable persons, volunteers, and beneficiaries.

(2) The officer shall maintain confidential reporting channels, coordinate safeguarding training, monitor protective policies, and ensure allegations are addressed through appropriate independent procedures.

(3) The officer has direct reporting access to the Board committee responsible for safeguarding.

## Part Five — Programmes and Institutions

### Article XI — The Seven Community Advancement Directorates

#### Section 1. Establishment and common mandate

(1) Afrovanguard shall maintain seven Community Advancement Directorates, each responsible for a distinct but complementary dimension of community transformation:

| Directorate | Focus |
|---|---|
| Street-to-Stardom (STS) | Children, parenting, and character formation |
| Africa GATES | Talent attraction, development, and projection |
| TECHOME | Technology, research, and engineering |
| MEDIAPRO | Media, design, and documentation |
| KCAP | Kingdoms, culture, languages, and land |
| BEC | Enterprise, project management, and analytics |
| Career Hub | Professional formation |

(2) Every Directorate shares one duty: **to preserve, project, or protect the values of the community while developing the capacities of its people.**

(3) Every Directorate shall operate under an approved charter, annual budget, performance framework, safeguarding policy, and succession plan, and is led under Article XIII.

(4) Attendance or reach figures alone do not constitute proof of impact for any Directorate.

#### Section 2. Street-to-Stardom (STS)

(1) **Mandate.** STS develops children and families at the grassroots, preserving the foundational values of the community through education, parenting, mentorship, character formation, and access to opportunity.

(2) **Functions.** STS shall:

- (a) identify children facing educational, social, developmental, or economic disadvantage;
- (b) provide structured learning, mentorship, and talent-discovery opportunities;
- (c) support parents and caregivers through parenting education and family-development programmes;
- (d) promote the seven foundational values among children and families;
- (e) run school and community outreach programmes;
- (f) support children in developing literacy, numeracy, creativity, social responsibility, and leadership;
- (g) coordinate age-appropriate life-skills and character-development initiatives;
- (h) promote safe environments and child safeguarding;
- (i) refer children and families needing specialised services to qualified providers; and
- (j) support the transition of eligible children into NGGC, Academy programmes, and other opportunities.

(3) **Performance indicators.** Children reached; children completing programmes; parenting sessions delivered; learning outcomes; safeguarding compliance; school partnerships; documented developmental progress.

#### Section 3. Africa GATES

(1) **Mandate.** Africa GATES identifies, attracts, develops, manages, and projects the talents of African communities in ways that strengthen communal identity, excellence, opportunity, and responsible representation.

(2) **Functions.** Africa GATES shall:

- (a) identify emerging and established talents across the community;
- (b) establish talent-development pathways and mentorship programmes;
- (c) coordinate exhibitions, showcases, competitions, and community festivals;
- (d) facilitate ethical representation and professional opportunities for talents;
- (e) support the creative and performing arts, innovation, leadership, and other talent sectors;
- (f) develop talent portfolios, records, and progression plans;
- (g) partner with educational, cultural, creative, and professional institutions;
- (h) promote positive community identity through the achievements of local talents;
- (i) ensure fair contracts, intellectual property protection, and transparent talent-management arrangements; and
- (j) develop successors and community-based talent managers.

(3) **Safeguards.** No talent shall be exploited through undisclosed commissions, coercive representation, misuse of intellectual property, or unauthorised commercial arrangements. Special protections apply to minors.

(4) **Performance indicators.** Talents discovered; talents trained; verified opportunities created; sustainable career outcomes; partnership value; participant satisfaction; ethical compliance.

#### Section 4. TECHOME

(1) **Mandate.** TECHOME advances the scientific and technological capacity of African communities through research, engineering, innovation, and responsible technological development, and ensures that technology strengthens rather than undermines the values, safety, autonomy, and long-term prosperity of the community.

(2) **Functions.** TECHOME shall:

- (a) establish technology learning and research programmes;
- (b) develop software engineering and digital product capabilities;
- (c) promote robotics, electronics, automation, and applied engineering;
- (d) facilitate responsible biotechnology and life-sciences education and research;
- (e) support artificial intelligence, data science, cybersecurity, and emerging technologies;
- (f) develop community-centred technological solutions;
- (g) promote ethical innovation and responsible research;
- (h) establish laboratories, makerspaces, technical academies, and research partnerships where financially and legally feasible;
- (i) support commercialisation and intellectual property development through approved arrangements; and
- (j) protect the community against technological exploitation, digital exclusion, unsafe research, and misuse of personal information.

(3) **Safeguards.** Research involving human participants, biological materials, medical claims, environmental risks, or sensitive data requires applicable ethical review, professional supervision, regulatory authorisation, and safety controls. No experimental research shall be conducted merely because Afrovanguard has the technical capacity to do so.

(4) **Performance indicators.** Learners trained; prototypes developed; research outputs; technical certifications; solutions deployed; partnerships; intellectual property outcomes; safety compliance.

#### Section 5. MEDIAPRO

(1) **Mandate.** MEDIAPRO is Afrovanguard's creative production, media development, visual communication, storytelling, and institutional documentation Directorate.

(2) **Functions.** MEDIAPRO shall:

- (a) produce graphic design and visual communication materials;
- (b) develop photography, cinematography, animation, audio, and video productions;
- (c) document programmes and community impact;
- (d) maintain approved digital media and content archives;
- (e) support public education, community storytelling, and cultural documentation;
- (f) train emerging media professionals;
- (g) produce institutional documentaries and educational media;
- (h) support approved campaigns and public engagement;
- (i) maintain technical standards for production, editing, storage, and publication; and
- (j) protect intellectual property, participant privacy, and consent rights.

(3) **Safeguards.** MEDIAPRO shall not falsify impact statistics, manipulate beneficiary testimonies, or misrepresent institutional achievements. It works with the CCO, who oversees institutional messaging and brand policy.

(4) **Performance indicators.** Productions completed; content quality; projects documented; training outcomes; audience engagement; archive completeness; consent compliance; contribution to institutional objectives.

#### Section 6. KCAP — Kingdom and Cultural Advancement Projects

(1) **Mandate.** KCAP preserves, researches, documents, revitalises, and responsibly projects the cultural institutions, traditional kingdoms, languages, lifestyles, indigenous knowledge, land heritage, and communal practices of African peoples.

(2) **Functions.** KCAP shall:

- (a) research and document the histories of traditional kingdoms and communities;
- (b) preserve indigenous languages, oral traditions, and cultural knowledge;
- (c) promote cultural education and intergenerational knowledge transfer;
- (d) support traditional arts, crafts, architecture, music, and festivals;
- (e) document indigenous food systems, lifestyles, and health-related knowledge;
- (f) encourage evidence-based study of traditional health practices without promoting unsafe or unverified medical claims;
- (g) promote environmental stewardship, land heritage, and responsible community development;
- (h) build respectful partnerships with traditional institutions and cultural custodians;
- (i) develop archives, publications, exhibitions, and educational resources; and
- (j) protect community cultural heritage against unauthorised exploitation or misrepresentation.

(3) **Safeguards.** KCAP shall respect the rights of cultural communities and knowledge custodians, and shall not claim ownership of sacred, confidential, or communally held knowledge merely because it has been documented through an Afrovanguard project.

(4) **Performance indicators.** Languages documented; cultural programmes delivered; heritage materials preserved; community partnerships; research outputs; evidence of cultural transmission to younger generations.

#### Section 7. BEC — Business and Enterprise Centre

(1) **Mandate.** BEC promotes ethical enterprise, business competence, economic participation, project management, and evidence-based decision-making for the protection and advancement of community values.

(2) **Functions.** BEC shall:

- (a) train entrepreneurs and emerging business leaders;
- (b) support small and medium enterprises;
- (c) run business incubation and mentorship programmes;
- (d) teach project planning, budgeting, operations, and evaluation;
- (e) promote data literacy and business analytics;
- (f) support community market research and economic development studies;
- (g) facilitate ethical enterprise partnerships;
- (h) encourage responsible financial management and sustainable business models;
- (i) develop business-support services within lawful non-profit boundaries; and
- (j) promote enterprises that create employment, strengthen local value chains, and improve community welfare.

(3) **Safeguards.** BEC shall not use charitable assets to confer improper private benefit on selected businesses or individuals. Any commercial subsidiary, investment, or revenue-generating activity shall operate under approved legal, tax, governance, and conflict-of-interest arrangements.

(4) **Performance indicators.** Entrepreneurs trained; enterprises supported; verified jobs created; business survival rates; project outcomes; financial-literacy improvement; sustainable economic impact.

#### Section 8. Career Hub

(1) **Mandate.** Career Hub develops competent, ethical, globally competitive African professionals with a strong sense of cultural identity, communal responsibility, and professional excellence.

(2) **Functions.** Career Hub shall:

- (a) provide career guidance and professional orientation;
- (b) support internships, apprenticeships, and work-readiness programmes;
- (c) establish mentorship relationships with qualified professionals;
- (d) teach professional ethics, workplace communication, and leadership;
- (e) support access to professional certifications and further education;
- (f) facilitate career networks and employment partnerships;
- (g) promote entrepreneurship as a legitimate professional pathway;
- (h) develop professional communities committed to African advancement;
- (i) support career transitions and continuing professional development; and
- (j) encourage professionals to contribute expertise to community development.

(3) **Professional standards.** Career Hub promotes merit, competence, ethical practice, and professional accountability. "Afrocentric" means cultural rootedness and commitment to African advancement; it does not mean hostility towards other peoples or exclusion from global professional cooperation.

(4) **Performance indicators.** Participants trained; mentorships completed; internships secured; verified employment outcomes; professional certifications; career progression; community-service contributions.

#### Section 9. One ecosystem

(1) The seven Directorates operate as one ecosystem, not as competing departments. STS develops children and families; Africa GATES discovers and advances talent; TECHOME builds technological capacity; MEDIAPRO documents and communicates; KCAP preserves cultural inheritance; BEC develops enterprise; and Career Hub develops professionals.

(2) **The constitutional doctrine of Afrovanguard:** *We preserve through STS. We project through Africa GATES. We protect through TECHOME. We communicate through MEDIAPRO. We sustain our heritage through KCAP. We create prosperity through BEC. We raise professionals through Career Hub. And through Afrovanguard Academy, we reproduce the leadership that sustains them all.*

### Article XII — The Academy, NGGC, CACENTREs and Affiliated Institutions

#### Section 1. Afrovanguard Academy

(1) There shall be an Afrovanguard Academy, the principal internal leadership formation institution, responsible for the formal transmission of institutional philosophy, values, leadership competence, civic responsibility, and organisational knowledge.

(2) The Academy shall:

- (a) develop approved institutional curricula;
- (b) train Members and prospective leaders;
- (c) run the Two-Trainer Formation programme;
- (d) organise and evaluate the Evaluated Academy Sessions required for governing offices;
- (e) administer Rank assessments;
- (f) maintain leadership certification records;
- (g) develop teaching materials and institutional publications; and
- (h) support leadership succession across all Directorates.

(3) The Academy shall have a Director and a Deputy Director, appointed under the same qualifications and procedures as Project Directors under Article XIII.

#### Section 2. Next Generation Genius Club (NGGC)

(1) NGGC is a structured youth leadership, creativity, technology, business, and personal-development programme for children and adolescents, delivered through supervised learning, mentorship, practical projects, and character development.

(2) NGGC works closely with STS, Africa GATES, TECHOME, BEC, Career Hub, and the Academy.

(3) NGGC's operational rules shall set age groups, mentor qualifications, parental engagement, attendance, child protection, and learning-assessment standards.

#### Section 3. CACENTREs

(1) CACENTREs are community-based hubs through which Afrovanguard programmes are delivered.

(2) Each CACENTRE shall operate under an approved management structure, safeguarding system, financial controls, asset register, and programme plan.

(3) No CACENTRE is the private property of its local Director, donor, host, or any individual associated with its establishment.

#### Section 4. Affiliated institutions

(1) Afrovanguard may cooperate with educational, cultural, technological, professional, or commercial institutions, including independently registered entities.

(2) Every such relationship shall be governed by a written agreement defining ownership, intellectual property, finance, safeguarding, branding, conflicts of interest, and institutional independence.

(3) No affiliated entity is owned or controlled by Afrovanguard solely because it shares founders, staff, facilities, or programme objectives.

### Article XIII — Project Directors and the Succession System

#### Section 1. Project Directorate

(1) Each core project shall be headed by a Project Director responsible for achieving its approved mandate.

(2) Each Project Director shall be supported by:

- (a) a Deputy Director;
- (b) a Project Administrator;
- (c) a Finance Officer;
- (d) a Volunteer Coordinator;
- (e) programme or technical officers as required; and
- (f) designated safeguarding personnel wherever children or vulnerable persons are served.

(3) A shared-services arrangement may be approved where resources do not justify separate appointments, provided that financial controls and accountability remain effective.

#### Section 2. Qualifications

Every candidate for Project Director shall:

- (a) have at least twelve consecutive months of Verified Active Membership;
- (b) have completed Two-Trainer Formation;
- (c) have taught at least two Evaluated Academy Sessions;
- (d) demonstrate practical competence relevant to the Directorate;
- (e) have satisfactory ethical and service records;
- (f) complete leadership and safeguarding training appropriate to the office;
- (g) present an operational plan for the proposed term; and
- (h) meet the Rank requirement in Article V, Section 2(2).

#### Section 3. Term of office

(1) A Project Director serves a two-year term.

(2) A Project Director may be reappointed for one further consecutive term after a satisfactory performance review and a competitive appointment process.

(3) No Project Director acquires a permanent entitlement to office.

#### Section 4. The Two-Successor Obligation

(1) Every Project Director shall develop at least two qualified potential successors during each two-year term, according to the following timeline:

| Month of term | Requirement |
|---|---|
| By month 6 | Identify at least two potential successors and begin assigning progressive leadership responsibilities |
| By month 18 | At least two candidates have completed substantial supervised leadership assignments |
| By month 22 | Submit a written succession report: each candidate's qualifications, training records, performance assessments, and readiness for appointment |

(2) Failure to develop two candidates shall be reviewed as a leadership performance deficiency, with fair consideration of documented circumstances.

#### Section 5. No automatic succession

(1) Neither the Deputy Director nor any nominated candidate automatically succeeds the outgoing Project Director.

(2) Every permanent appointment follows transparent eligibility assessment and formal approval.

#### Section 6. Removal and vacancy

(1) A Project Director may be removed for serious misconduct, safeguarding violations, financial impropriety, persistent non-performance, or substantial breach of duty, following due process.

(2) On a vacancy, an eligible Acting Director may be appointed for up to six months, unless an extension is formally justified and approved.

(3) An acting appointment creates no permanent entitlement to office.

#### Section 7. Performance covenant

Every Project Director shall execute an annual performance covenant in the form set in Schedule C.

## Part Six — Integrity, Resources and Accountability

### Article XIV — Non-Hereditary Leadership and Founder Independence

#### Section 1. Foundational declaration

(1) Afrovanguard is a perpetual community institution and shall not be inherited by the Founder's family or by any other family.

(2) The offices of President, Trustee, Council Chairperson, Board Chairperson, and Project Director are not inheritable property.

#### Section 2. Prohibition of hereditary succession

(1) No spouse, child, sibling, parent, descendant, or other relative of the Founder shall acquire leadership authority through inheritance, family designation, personal instruction, or presumed entitlement.

(2) No institutional asset, membership right, or governance authority passes to the Founder's estate on death or incapacity.

#### Section 3. Independent appointment

(1) All leadership appointments shall be based on verified eligibility, competence, service, institutional formation, and the prescribed appointment process.

(2) A relative of the Founder may participate as an ordinary Member on equal terms, but shall receive no preferential treatment.

#### Section 4. Protection against family control

Afrovanguard shall maintain safeguards against direct or indirect family domination. The following are prohibited:

- (a) appointments designed to transfer institutional control to the Founder's family;
- (b) family voting blocs exercising undisclosed beneficial control;
- (c) appointment decisions involving undisclosed family conflicts of interest;
- (d) use of institutional assets for the private benefit of relatives; and
- (e) informal family instructions overriding constitutional decisions.

#### Section 5. The Founder's legacy

(1) The Founder's contributions shall be preserved through institutional archives, approved histories, educational materials, and the transmission of the foundational philosophy.

(2) Historical recognition confers no permanent executive power and no immunity from accountability.

### Article XV — Finance and Property

#### Section 1. Financial principles

(1) All income, property, and resources of Afrovanguard shall be applied exclusively to its lawful purposes.

(2) No part of its income or property shall be distributed as profit to Members, Trustees, Directors, or the Founder.

(3) Reasonable compensation for legitimate services may be paid where lawful, properly approved, documented, and free from improper conflicts of interest.

#### Section 2. Sources of income

Afrovanguard may receive income from:

- (a) membership dues;
- (b) voluntary donations;
- (c) grants and philanthropic support;
- (d) corporate sponsorships;
- (e) fundraising campaigns;
- (f) approved programme fees;
- (g) educational and training services;
- (h) lawful revenue-generating activities consistent with non-profit restrictions;
- (i) gifts, endowments, and bequests; and
- (j) other lawful sources approved under institutional policy.

#### Section 3. Annual budget

(1) The CFO shall prepare a consolidated annual budget with the COO, CPIO, Project Directors, and other responsible officers.

(2) The Board shall review and approve the budget, within its delegated authority, before the start of the financial year.

(3) No Directorate shall undertake a material unbudgeted commitment without the required authorisation.

#### Section 4. Accounts

(1) All institutional funds shall be held in approved accounts under the legal control of Afrovanguard.

(2) No Director, volunteer, officer, or Trustee shall collect institutional funds into a personal account, except under a narrowly defined, documented emergency procedure expressly permitted by law and policy.

(3) Every receipt shall be recorded in the approved financial system.

#### Section 5. Separation of duties

(1) Afrovanguard shall maintain separation between: payment initiation; budget authorisation; payment approval; payment execution; reconciliation; and independent review.

(2) No person shall exercise all of these functions over the same transaction.

#### Section 6. Procurement

(1) Procurement shall follow documented procedures proportionate to transaction value and risk.

(2) The Board shall approve financial thresholds for competitive quotations, tenders, emergency purchases, related-party transactions, and major capital expenditure.

(3) Any officer with a personal or financial interest in a procurement decision shall disclose the interest and recuse themselves where required.

#### Section 7. Restricted funds

(1) Funds received for a specified project or donor-approved purpose shall be recorded separately and used only in accordance with the restriction.

(2) Restricted funds shall not be redirected merely because another project is in financial difficulty.

#### Section 8. Reporting and audit

(1) The CFO shall prepare annual financial statements and coordinate the independent review or audit required by law and institutional policy.

(2) The Board shall receive quarterly financial reports, and the General Assembly an annual financial accountability report.

#### Section 9. Membership dues and contributions

(1) Membership dues and governance contributions shall be set in the Financial Schedule. The proposed amounts are in Schedule E.

(2) Dues for ordinary membership classes may be made mandatory once approved.

(3) Higher amounts listed for governing officers, Patrons, and Grand Patrons are fundraising expectations or voluntary pledges, unless a separate lawful and equitable policy provides otherwise.

(4) Financial capacity shall never replace merit, institutional service, or the mandatory qualification pathway.

### Article XVI — Partnerships and Affiliations

#### Section 1. Authority

(1) Afrovanguard may enter into partnerships with governments, educational institutions, businesses, civil society organisations, traditional institutions, research organisations, professional associations, and international development partners.

(2) Every partnership shall advance Afrovanguard's lawful purposes.

#### Section 2. Review

Every material partnership shall be reviewed for:

- (a) mission alignment;
- (b) legal and reputational risk;
- (c) financial obligations;
- (d) safeguarding implications;
- (e) data protection and confidentiality;
- (f) intellectual property;
- (g) independence and conflicts of interest; and
- (h) measurable community benefit.

#### Section 3. Prohibition of improper influence

(1) No partner, donor, or Patron acquires authority to appoint Trustees, Directors, or executive officers by providing financial support.

(2) No partnership shall require Afrovanguard to abandon its constitutional values or misrepresent its activities.

#### Section 4. International partnerships

International engagements shall comply with applicable Nigerian and foreign law, including rules on grants, employment, taxation, data transfers, research, and charitable activities.

### Article XVII — Ethics, Safeguarding and Discipline

#### Section 1. General duty

Every Member and officeholder shall act honestly, responsibly, respectfully, and in accordance with the foundational values.

#### Section 2. Serious misconduct

The following constitute serious misconduct:

- (a) fraud, theft, bribery, or misappropriation;
- (b) abuse or exploitation of children or vulnerable persons;
- (c) sexual harassment or coercion;
- (d) retaliation against whistleblowers;
- (e) deliberate falsification of financial or impact records;
- (f) undisclosed self-dealing or improper private benefit;
- (g) unauthorised disclosure or misuse of confidential information;
- (h) abuse of office or discriminatory treatment;
- (i) serious misuse of institutional property; and
- (j) obstruction of a legitimate investigation.

#### Section 3. Child safeguarding

(1) Every programme involving children shall implement appropriate safeguarding policies, including screening of relevant personnel, codes of conduct, reporting procedures, consent requirements, and referral protocols.

(2) No leader's Rank, reputation, or financial contribution exempts them from safeguarding procedures.

#### Section 4. Whistleblower protection

(1) Members, staff, volunteers, and beneficiaries shall be able to report suspected misconduct through confidential and accessible channels.

(2) No good-faith report shall result in retaliation.

#### Section 5. Independent investigation

(1) Serious allegations against senior officers shall be referred to an independent committee or a qualified external investigator where necessary.

(2) No accused person shall control the investigation into their own conduct.

#### Section 6. Due process

(1) No Member shall be removed or sanctioned arbitrarily.

(2) Every disciplinary procedure shall provide notice, a fair opportunity to respond, impartial consideration, and an appropriate right of appeal.

### Article XVIII — Research, Data and Intellectual Property

#### Section 1. Research integrity

(1) All institutional research shall comply with applicable law, ethical standards, and professional requirements.

(2) Research results shall not be fabricated, falsified, or selectively misrepresented.

#### Section 2. Data governance

(1) Afrovanguard shall maintain policies governing the collection, storage, use, sharing, retention, and deletion of personal data, consistent with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

(2) Special safeguards apply to children's information, financial records, health-related data, and sensitive research materials.

#### Section 3. Intellectual property

(1) Intellectual property created through institutional projects shall be governed by written agreements defining ownership, licensing, attribution, compensation where applicable, and community rights.

(2) No officer shall appropriate project intellectual property for personal use without authorisation.

#### Section 4. Indigenous knowledge

Research involving indigenous cultural knowledge shall respect community custodianship, informed participation, cultural sensitivity, and applicable intellectual property protections.

#### Section 5. Technology ethics

(1) TECHOME and every other technology programme shall prioritise human dignity, safety, fairness, privacy, transparency, and community benefit.

(2) No technology shall be deployed solely because it is technically possible.

### Article XIX — Performance and Public Accountability

#### Section 1. Annual institutional report

The President, through the COO, CFO, and CPIO, shall present an annual report covering:

- (a) programme activities and outcomes;
- (b) financial performance;
- (c) membership and volunteer development;
- (d) safeguarding and risk management;
- (e) leadership succession;
- (f) institutional partnerships;
- (g) research and innovation;
- (h) strategic progress; and
- (i) material challenges and corrective actions.

#### Section 2. Measuring impact

(1) Afrovanguard shall distinguish between activities, outputs, outcomes, and long-term impact.

(2) The number of people reached shall not be reported as the number of people transformed.

#### Section 3. Institutional dashboard

(1) Afrovanguard shall maintain an approved management information system for attendance, programme delivery, training, finance, assets, volunteer development, leadership progression, and performance reporting.

(2) Access shall be role-based and subject to privacy and security controls.

#### Section 4. Independent evaluation

(1) Major programmes shall undergo periodic independent evaluation proportionate to their scale, risk, and funding.

(2) Afrovanguard shall publish appropriate summaries of findings and corrective actions.

## Part Seven — Appointments and Continuity

### Article XX — Elections, Appointments and Removal

#### Section 1. Nominations

(1) The Governance, Nominations and Succession Committee verifies the eligibility of candidates for governing offices.

(2) The Committee shall include members independent of the candidate under consideration.

#### Section 2. Candidate verification

(1) No candidate shall be presented for appointment without a verified qualification record.

(2) For the Board and registered Trustees, the record shall include evidence of twelve months' Verified Active Membership, Two-Trainer Formation certification, and two Evaluated Academy Sessions.

(3) For the Governing Council, the record shall additionally show the qualifications in Article VII, Section 3, including Rank D.

#### Section 3. Elections

(1) Elections shall be conducted through transparent procedures set in the Election Bylaws.

(2) Candidates shall have equal access to nomination and voting procedures.

(3) No candidate shall purchase votes, intimidate voters, or misuse institutional resources for electioneering.

#### Section 4. Conflicts of interest

No person shall take part in deciding their own appointment, remuneration, disciplinary case, or related-party transaction.

#### Section 5. Removal

(1) An officer may be removed for legally recognised disqualification, serious misconduct, persistent non-performance, incapacity, or substantial breach of constitutional duty.

(2) Removal shall follow the procedure prescribed for the office concerned in this Constitution and the Bylaws, and shall comply with applicable law.

(3) Registered Trustees shall be removed or replaced only through legally valid procedures.

### Article XXI — Tenure, Succession and Handover

#### Section 1. Terms of office

Terms of office and consecutive-term limits are consolidated in Schedule D. Where Schedule D and an Article differ, the Article prevails.

#### Section 2. Succession planning

(1) Every governing and executive body shall maintain an approved succession plan.

(2) The President and every Chief Officer shall maintain succession and continuity plans appropriate to their responsibilities.

(3) The Two-Successor Obligation for Project Directors is set in Article XIII, Section 4.

#### Section 3. Emergency continuity

(1) Where an office becomes vacant, the competent governing authority shall appoint an eligible acting officer for a defined transitional period.

(2) Emergency appointments shall not waive the permanent eligibility requirements for registered Trustees or voting Directors.

#### Section 4. Handover

Every outgoing officer shall deliver all institutional property, records, accounts, passwords, contracts, project documentation, and pending matters through a documented handover process.

## Part Eight — Final Provisions

### Article XXII — Bylaws and Subsidiary Instruments

#### Section 1. Required instruments

The following instruments shall be made under this Constitution:

| Instrument | Governs | Approving body |
|---|---|---|
| Membership Bylaws | Membership classes, admission, voting eligibility, Verified Active Membership | Board, ratified by General Assembly |
| Election Bylaws | Notice, quorum, proxy and electronic voting, elections, removal procedures | General Assembly |
| Rank and Formation Schedule | Rank competencies, training, thresholds, promotion | Governing Council, on Academy recommendation |
| Financial Schedule | Dues, contributions, spending thresholds | Board |
| Delegated Authority Bylaws | Detailed allocation of powers (see Schedule A) | Board, reconciled with Trustees |
| Directorate Charters | Mandate and operations of each Directorate | Board |

#### Section 2. Consistency

No Bylaw or subsidiary instrument may contradict this Constitution. To the extent of any inconsistency, this Constitution prevails.

### Article XXIII — Amendment

#### Section 1. Power of amendment

This Constitution may be amended only through the procedure in this Article and in compliance with applicable law.

#### Section 2. Proposal

An amendment may be proposed by:

- (a) a resolution of the Governing Council;
- (b) a resolution of the Board;
- (c) the registered Trustees acting within their lawful powers; or
- (d) a petition supported by at least one-third of eligible voting Members.

#### Section 3. Notice

(1) A proposed amendment shall be circulated to eligible voting Members with sufficient notice, as set in the Election Bylaws, to permit meaningful consideration.

(2) The notice shall identify the existing provision, the proposed replacement, the reasons for the change, and its anticipated implications.

#### Section 4. Approval

(1) An amendment requires approval by at least two-thirds of votes cast at a duly convened General Assembly, subject to any higher threshold or different procedure required by law or the Registered Constitution.

(2) Where CAC approval or filing is required, the amendment takes legal effect only in accordance with that process.

#### Section 5. Protected principles

(1) No amendment shall convert Afrovanguard into inheritable family property, authorise unlawful private distribution of charitable assets, or abolish accountability for institutional misconduct.

(2) Any amendment affecting Afrovanguard's non-profit character, trustee powers, or asset-distribution restrictions requires legal review before it is considered.

### Article XXIV — Dissolution

#### Section 1. Procedure

Afrovanguard may be dissolved only through the lawful procedure applicable to its registered status.

#### Section 2. Liabilities

On dissolution, lawful debts and liabilities shall be settled before any remaining assets are distributed.

#### Section 3. Remaining assets

(1) Any remaining assets shall be transferred to one or more eligible non-profit institutions with substantially similar purposes, in accordance with applicable law and any required regulatory or court approval.

(2) No remaining assets shall be distributed as private property among the Founder, relatives, Members, Trustees, Directors, or Patrons.

### Article XXV — Ratification and Transitional Provisions

#### Section 1. Ratification

This Constitution becomes Afrovanguard's operative constitution only on completion of the required approval and legal procedures, recorded in Schedule G.

#### Section 2. Existing appointments

(1) Existing lawful appointments are not invalidated by the adoption of new qualification standards.

(2) The transition shall preserve legal continuity while setting clear deadlines for compliance, reappointment, or succession.

#### Section 3. The incumbent President

A transitional resolution may recognise the incumbent Founder's prior service and fix the date on which the first term under this Constitution begins, subject to lawful adoption.

#### Section 4. Implementation within 120 days

Within 120 days of lawful adoption, Afrovanguard shall establish or confirm:

- (a) the General Assembly voting register;
- (b) the composition of the Governing Council and the Board;
- (c) the registered Trustee records;
- (d) executive office descriptions and appointments;
- (e) the seven Directorate charters;
- (f) the Academy qualification and Rank assessment system;
- (g) the Two-Successor leadership development programme; and
- (h) financial, safeguarding, data, and conflict-of-interest policies.

#### Section 5. Existing programmes

(1) Existing programmes, including NGGC, STS, and CACENTRE operations, continue under approved transitional arrangements.

(2) No programme shall be terminated solely because of a change in constitutional structure without an appropriate review of beneficiaries, obligations, staff, funding, and operational continuity.

## Explanatory Note — Implementation Matters

*This note is not part of the Constitution.*

The ultimate test of this Constitution will not be the sophistication of its language. It will be whether Afrovanguard can operate transparently, protect children, manage resources responsibly, develop capable leaders, and survive a complete change of leadership without losing its purpose.

This Constitution shall be read together with the Registered Constitution filed with the CAC and kept under review by qualified Nigerian non-profit counsel. The following matters are to be settled in the Bylaws:

1. **Allocation of supreme authority.** The Governing Council, registered Trustees, and Board cannot each hold unrestricted authority. The Delegated Authority Bylaws must allocate their powers without conflict (see Schedule A and the Delegated Authority Bylaws).
2. **Office of Secretary.** Schedule G calls for a Secretary's signature, but no secretarial office is established. Decide whether to create a Company Secretary / General Secretary office (statutory filings, minutes, registers) and where it reports.
3. **Body that removes Council members and appoints Trustees.** Article VII, Section 7 and Schedule A refer to a "competent" body. The Election Bylaws must name it.
4. **Notice period for amendments.** Article XXIII, Section 3 requires "sufficient notice". A fixed period (for example, 30 days) is recommended.
5. **Restriction on Founder's relatives.** A proposed further restriction would make the Founder's relatives ineligible for the Presidency and the chair of the principal governing bodies. It has been left out of the operative text pending legal review of its enforceability and fairness.
6. **Governance contributions.** Schedule E lists contributions for offices (Project Director, Director, Trustee, Council) alongside membership classes. Counsel should confirm these cannot operate as a pay-to-serve condition, consistent with Article IV, Section 4(2) and Article XV, Section 9(4).
7. **Affordability of executive offices.** Article X, Section 3(4) now lets one person hold more than one Chief Officer role until separate appointments are affordable, with independence kept for finance, compliance, and safeguarding.

### Changes made in this edition

- Introduced Parts, a definitions article (Article II), and a uniform numbering scheme: Article → Section → numbered clause (1) → lettered paragraph (a).
- Gave every Directorate the same structure: Mandate, Functions, Safeguards, Performance indicators.
- Removed duplication: Board/Trustee eligibility now refers back to Article IV; Rank requirements for office are in one place (Article V, Section 2); terms of office are consolidated in Schedule D.
- Moved the President's transitional provision to Article XXV, the dues table to Schedule E, and the ecosystem commentary and constitutional doctrine into Article XI, Section 9.
- Added Article XXII (Bylaws and Subsidiary Instruments), listing every instrument the text relies on.
- Turned the review commentary into this Explanatory Note, outside the operative text.
- Standardised terms ("President" for "Chief Executive or President"; "Chief Officers" for "Chief Executive Officers"; "Verified Active Membership"; "Two-Trainer Formation") and drafting voice ("shall" for duties, "may" for powers).
- Added the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 reference in Article XVIII and the CAMA 2020 reference in Article VIII.
