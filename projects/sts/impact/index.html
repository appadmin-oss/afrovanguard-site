How we measure

Every program in our portfolio is bound by the same five-pillar rhythm: diagnose, design, deliver, document, distribute. The measurement work happens at the diagnose and document ends — and we use the same instruments year-on-year so that the numbers are comparable across cycles.

For LCASP and Summer School, we administer a baseline assessment within the first week of intake and an endline within the last week of the same term. Both use a partner-verified instrument adapted from the EGRA (Early Grade Reading Assessment) framework. For NextGen Genius Club, the unit of measurement is project completion and public-exhibition presentation — we record both per-child. For STREET Storm, we follow each participant for six months post-program and record their employment, apprenticeship or further-study status.

Where we have revised a previously-published number, the case-study table flags it. Where a pilot did not yield a statistically significant gain, we publish the finding and retire the pilot.

What we don't claim

We do not control for prior schooling outside our partner schools. We do not claim that improvements in our programs are causally attributable to our work — only that the children in our cohorts moved between baseline and endline. Where appropriate we discuss confounding factors in the case-study notes.
